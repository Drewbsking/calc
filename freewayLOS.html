Basic Freeway LOS Calculator

HCM 6th Edition (2016), Chapter 12

Analyze one direction of a basic freeway segment with two or more lanes. For a highway with at-grade access, use the multilane calculator (2010 / 6th Edition).

Starting inputs are examples; replace them with site data.

Base conditions: speed and capacity adjustment factors are 1.00. This method does not model ramp junctions, weaving, queues, managed lanes, weather, or incidents.

Terrain and heavy vehicles

Calculation formulas

HCM 6th Edition basic freeway method, with SAF = CAF = 1. Keep full precision until displaying results.

1. Free-flow speed (Eq. 12-2)

FFS = BFFS − fLW − fRLC − 3.22 × TRD0.84

BFFS is base free-flow speed; TRD is ramps per mile. Use measured FFS directly when available.

Lane penalty fLW = 0 at widths ≥12 ft, 1.9 mph for 11–<12 ft, and 6.6 mph for 10–<11 ft (Exhibit 12-20).

fRLC = [6 − min(clearance, 6)] × k

Exhibit 12-21 is linear between clearance rows: k = 0.6, 0.4, 0.2, or 0.1 mph/ft for 2, 3, 4, or ≥5 directional lanes, respectively.

2. Heavy vehicles and flow (Eqs. 12-9, 12-10)

fHV = 1 / [1 + PT(ET − 1)]
vp = V / (PHF × N × fHV)

PT is the heavy-vehicle percentage divided by 100; ET is its passenger-car equivalent (2 for level terrain, 3 for rolling terrain, or a specific-grade lookup). V is directional demand in veh/h; N is directional lanes; vp is passenger-car flow in pc/h/ln.

3. Capacity, speed, density (Eq. 12-6, Eq. 12-1, Exhibit 12-6)

c = min[2,200 + 10(FFS − 50), 2,400]
BP = 1,000 + 40(75 − FFS)

Capacity c and speed-curve breakpoint BP are in pc/h/ln. The supported FFS range is 55–75 mph.

S = FFS, when vp ≤ BP
S = FFS − (FFS − c/45) × [(vp − BP)/(c − BP)]2, when BP < vp ≤ c
D = vp / S

S is average speed in mph; D is density in pc/mi/ln. Demand above capacity is LOS F; the basic segment method does not predict speed or density in that condition.
