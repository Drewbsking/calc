Start with the typical number, then read the codes separated by hyphens. Codes describe the roadway, work, location, or traffic control. Their order and combination vary by detail. The MDOT code filter matches codes throughout the filename. Numbered variants share their base code: 1LC and 2LC match LC; CLT(7) matches CLT; 2(R)SHIFT matches SHIFT and (R). A direction code can describe a closure or a shift; review the drawing.

Numbers, parentheses, and letter suffixes

  • Typical number: The current construction series uses 100–380, maintenance uses 4000-series numbers, and survey uses 5000-series numbers.
  • Lane counts and sides: 2(R+L)LC means two lane closures, one right and one left. (1-2)LC means one- to two-lane closure. 1(CLT) identifies a center left-turn lane closure.
  • Shift counts: 2(R)SHIFT and SHIFT-2(R) describe two lanes shifted right; 1(L)SHIFT describes one lane shifted left.
  • Crossover counts: The final (1) in 311 and (2) in 312 identify single and dual crossover closures.
  • A / B after the number: Different versions of that typical. The distinction depends on the typical: 4110A/B differ in speed reduction, while 5182A/B differ in traffic-control and traffic-volume conditions. Read the title and drawing for the specific version.
