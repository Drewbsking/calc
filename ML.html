Multilane Highway LOS Calculator

HCM 6th Edition (2016), Chapter 12: automobile LOS on a four-lane highway with two lanes in each direction and uninterrupted flow.

Traffic

Compare upgrade and downgrade conditions using the same directional demand. Enter the volume for one direction, across both lanes.

Include all trucks, buses and RVs. In this edition, buses and RVs count as single-unit trucks (SUTs).

General terrain applies where no individual grade controls operations. Level terrain permits heavy vehicles to maintain passenger-car speeds; rolling terrain slows trucks without sustained crawl speeds.

Uniform grade

SUT = single-unit truck (including buses and RVs); TT = tractor-trailer. These percentages describe the heavy vehicles, not all traffic. The 30% SUT mix is more common on rural roads, 50% on urban roads; use 70% where SUTs predominate.

Automatic table lookup covers signed grades from -2% to +6%, lengths of at least 660 ft (0.125 mi), and at least 2% heavy vehicles. Longer grades use the last tabulated length; 25% and above use the final percentage column. Zero heavy vehicles needs no PCE adjustment.

Measured speed

Use a representative passenger-car speed study under low to moderate flow (up to 500 pc/h/ln). Roadway adjustments are already reflected in measured FFS.

Estimate free-flow speed

Speed under base geometric conditions, before the adjustments below; this is not the posted speed limit.

Clearance is measured from the edge of the travel lanes. Each side is capped at 6 ft. For undivided highways and two-way left-turn lanes, the method uses 6 ft on the left.

Count driveways and unsignalized intersections that influence traffic. The speed adjustment reaches its maximum at 40 access points per mile.

FFS must be from 45 to 70 mph. HCM 6th Edition uses the measured or estimated speed directly, including intermediate values such as 56 mph.

Method, sources and scope

Applies to uninterrupted automobile flow. Use a separate analysis for signal effects, queues from downstream bottlenecks, or composite grades. Run separately when traffic or FFS differs by direction. For steep grades or high truck percentages, HCM recommends its mixed-flow model for more detailed speed and density estimates.

Speed is reduced above 1,400 pc/h/ln. Demand above the selected curve's capacity is LOS F; speed and density are not predicted for that condition. Density is expressed in passenger cars per mile per lane (pc/mi/ln).
